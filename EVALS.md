# EVALS.md

The grading contract for AI generated analyst notes.

Player pages state that grounding is graded here before any note goes live. This document is that gate. Nothing generated by a model appears in the product until it has been graded against the criteria below by a human, cell by cell.

**Current status: no notes are live.** Two generation passes are complete and graded against identical payloads. A third is queued against corrected payloads, see section 7.2. Section 9 states which properties of this process the code enforces and which are conventions. `AiTrace` renders a placeholder on every player page until that changes.

---

## 1. Why this exists

Every other surface in this product states its thresholds, shows whether a reading has evidence behind it, and says plainly when it does not know. Generated text is the one place where a claim can appear that traces to nothing. A note that reads well and is wrong is worse than no note, because the rest of the site has spent its credibility telling the reader that claims here are checkable.

So the notes are held behind a gate rather than shipped and monitored.

## 2. The grounding contract

Each note is generated from a single payload and may assert nothing outside it. The payload contains, for one player: the window start and end dates, ADP at both ends and the move between them, ECR at both ends and the move between them, the gap between ADP and ECR, the signal label, the published thresholds, the calendar phase and its trust level, and any catalysts inside the window with their date, category, verification state and summary.

Notes are descriptive. They report what has already happened. They do not forecast, recommend, or advise.

## 3. Failure modes

A note passes only if it passes all five. Any single failure fails the note. Partial grading produces no verdict at all, and no score is computed from an incomplete grade.

| # | Failure mode | Fails when | Passes when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ungrounded claim | The note states a fact not present in the payload, such as a team, an injury, a role, a quote, a transaction or a standing, or contradicts a field the payload does supply | Every factual assertion traces to a field in the payload, and none contradicts one |
| 2 | Numeric drift | Any figure is misquoted, mis-signed, or stated in the wrong direction. A positive ADP move means drafted earlier, which is more expensive | All figures match the payload exactly and every direction is stated correctly |
| 3 | Causal overreach | The note asserts a cause the data does not support, including attributing an unchanged price to a catalyst that happens to sit in the window | Events and moves are described as concurrent, or a catalyst is named as context without a causal claim |
| 4 | Prediction | The note forecasts, or makes a draft recommendation of any kind | The note describes only what has already happened |
| 5 | Hedge mismatch | Confidence does not match the evidence available: a flat assertion on an unexplained move, or unnecessary hedging where a verified catalyst sits in the window | Language tracks the evidence tier |

**Vocabulary.** Every payload carries a `signal` field and that field is always a label. A note may name the label and may state that movement fell below a published threshold. It may never state that no signal exists or that nothing registered as a signal.

**Edge cases.** A correct refusal to generate counts as a pass on all five. Restating the signal label counts as grounded, because the label is in the payload. Describing the sign of the gap is not a prediction; telling the reader what to do about it is.

## 4. Adversarial cases

Three of the thirteen are constructed to be failed. They test the behaviours that matter most, which are the ones where saying less is correct.

| Case | Input | Correct output |
|---|---|---|
| Flat non mover | Both series unchanged across the window, with a verified catalyst on file | States that there is no significant movement to explain, and explicitly declines to attribute the flat price to the available catalyst |
| Mover with no verified catalyst | A real move above threshold, nothing documented in the window | Describes the move and supplies no cause |
| Sub threshold noise | Movement below the published bar | Names the assigned label, states that neither series cleared its bar, and generates no reading. It must not claim that no signal exists |

## 5. Procedure

1. Generate notes for the current movers plus the three adversarial cases, with the exact payload recorded alongside each note.
2. A human grades every note against all five failure modes, one cell each, and records a short reason for any failure.
3. Grades are written to `data/note_grades.json`. The scorecard on the methodology page reads that file and renders an honest empty state until every cell is marked.
4. Where notes fail, the fix is to the grounding contract, not to the individual notes. Name the failure class, change the contract, regenerate the full set, and grade again.
5. Both grading passes are kept, so the before and after is visible rather than asserted.

Nothing is cached or rendered in the product until step 4 comes back clean.

## 6. Regression policy

The graded set is the golden set. Any change to the grounding contract or the generation prompt is re-run against these same payloads and regraded before it ships. A note that passes safety but fails usefulness is caught here rather than in production.

## 7. Open

Two gaps are known and neither is in force. Both were found by a human reading the output, which is the only instrument that reaches them.

**7.1 Usefulness.** A sixth check is under consideration: whether a note supplies anything beyond a paraphrase of its own inputs. Reading the generated set before grading is what raised it. The five checks above test whether a note is false. They do not test whether it is worth reading, and a note that restates the numbers already displayed next to it passes every one of them while adding nothing.

If adopted, it becomes a sixth column on the scorecard and a required interpretive clause in the contract, tied to the calendar phase or the evidence tier.

**7.2 Payload defect.** Every check in section 3 grades a note against its payload. Nothing grades the payload against the world. A note that faithfully reports an incomplete or incorrect payload therefore passes all five, and the rubric cannot tell the difference between a note that is right and a note that is only obedient.

This is not hypothetical. Note 5 states, verbatim, "A verified catalyst sits inside the window: on 2026-08-11 he signed a one-year deal with the Washington Commanders." The signing was reported on 08-05 and announced by the club on 08-07. The note passed check 1 in both passes and its grader note calls it the strongest catalyst handling in the non adversarial set. It was grounded, and it was wrong, because its payload was wrong.

The defect traces to the verification procedure rather than to any grader. `data/catalysts.json` defines `verified: true` as a human confirming both the event and its date, but nothing required the cited source to display a date. The source behind that entry renders `Published:` and `Updated:` with empty date fields. A dateless source cannot verify a date.

The same blind spot covers omission as well as error. Eleven of the thirteen payloads carried no catalyst at all. A search of the eval set on 2026-08-14 found a real, datable, independently corroborated event for all twelve distinct players, none of which were in the file. "No verified catalyst is on file" was true of the file and misleading about the world, and no check could see the gap.

Two changes follow, and neither is a rubric change, which is the point:

1. A source must display a publication date for that date to count as verified. Entries carry `date_confirmed_on_source`, and entries predating the rule are marked unknown pending audit.
2. Entries carry `sources[]` with an independent corroboration count. Independence means a distinct newsroom, not syndication of one wire item. The count is a proxy for reach, and reach is the mechanism by which news moves an ADP built from thousands of drafts.

A sixth and seventh check would not have caught this. Grading harder was never the fix; the input was.

## 8. Results

**Pass 1, 2026-08-14. Thirteen notes, twelve pass, one fail. Zero live.**

The single failure was note 13, the sub threshold noise case. It stated that the movement "does not register as a signal" while the `signal` field it was handed read `Broad agreement`, which the product renders as a chip beside the note. The note contradicted its own input and would have contradicted the page around it.

The note was also compliant. Section 4 of this document, as written for pass 1, gave the correct output for that case as "reports that this does not register as a signal." The generator did what the contract asked. The defect was in the specification, not the generation, and grading it as a note failure alone would have left the cause in place and reproduced it on every future run.

Check 1 was also unable to hold the failure cleanly. Contradicting a supplied field is not the same as inventing an absent one, and checks 2 through 5 do not reach it, so pass 1 could only catch it by stretching check 1 past its stated definition.

**Pass 2, 2026-08-14. Thirteen notes, thirteen pass. Zero live.**

Regenerated against byte identical payloads under the amended contract. Twelve notes are unchanged, so the fix is isolated to the case it targeted and introduced no regressions. Note 13 now names its assigned label instead of denying that a label exists, which was verified against the live page: the player carries a `Broad agreement` chip, so the note and the page now agree.

Machine readable grades: `data/note_grades.json`. Rendered scorecard: the methodology page.

## 9. What is enforced, and what is convention

This document describes a process. Most of that process is people doing things carefully. A small part of it is now the code refusing to do otherwise, and it is worth being exact about which is which, because "gated behind evals" implies more machinery than exists.

**Enforced by code, as of 2026-08-15.**

- *A note passes only if it passes all five.* `lib/evals.ts` `derivePass()` returns the AND of the five cells, and the scorecard renders that, not the `pass` field recorded in the file. Where the two differ the page shows the derived verdict and flags the disagreement in red rather than preferring either silently.
- *Partial grading produces no verdict.* The scorecard's empty state keys on whether every cell carries a value, not on whether a result object exists. A result typed into `data/note_grades.json` over blank cells will not render as a score. `derivePass()` returns null for an incomplete row rather than falling through to a pass.
- *The five columns are the five columns.* `lib/evals.test.ts` asserts every column key exists on every row of every pass, and that no row carries a key outside them. A renamed key previously rendered as an ungraded dot with no error; it now fails the test run.
- *The recorded numbers match the cells.* The same test recomputes each pass's totals from its rows and fails if they diverge from what is written in the file.

**Convention, with no mechanism behind it.**

- *A human graded every cell.* `graded_by` is a string in a JSON file. Nothing in the codebase can tell a hand grade from a pasted one. This is the load-bearing claim of the whole harness and it rests entirely on the honesty of the person maintaining it.
- *The regression set is re-run before a prompt change ships.* Section 6 states the policy. No test enforces it, no CI checks it, and there is no generation pipeline to hook it to. The payloads have in fact stayed byte-identical across both passes, which is checkable in the file and was checked, but nothing would have caught it if they had not.
- *Nothing is rendered until the gate comes back clean.* True today because no generation pipeline exists. There is no code path that would refuse to render an ungraded note, because there is no code path that renders a note at all.
- *The rubric on the page and the rubric in this document say the same thing.* They are two hand-maintained copies. The page renders `_schema.columns` from the JSON; section 3 above is separate prose. Nothing compares them.

The honest one-line version: the scorecard cannot invent a score or hide a contradiction, and everything else about this process is a person being careful in public.

## 10. Change log

**Pass 1 to pass 2, 2026-08-14.** Three changes, all to this document, none to any note.

- Section 3 gained the vocabulary clause. Cause: note 13.
- Section 3 check 1 widened to cover contradiction as well as fabrication. Cause: note 13 had no correct cell.
- Section 4 corrected the stated correct output for the sub threshold case, which had instructed the behaviour that failed.

The payload set is unchanged, per the regression policy in section 6.

**Pass 2 to pass 3, 2026-08-14.** Queued, not run. The payload defect in section 7.2 invalidates the catalyst field of every pass 2 payload: one was wrong and eleven were incomplete. Pass 3 regenerates against corrected payloads once the new catalyst entries carry human verification, which is the one step in this document an agent is not permitted to perform. Passes 1 and 2 stay frozen and graded, per section 5 step 5.

**Known weaknesses carried into pass 2, documented rather than hidden.**

- The sixth check in section 7 is still unadopted. Eleven of the thirteen restate their payloads and add nothing a reader could not compute from the numbers beside them. Notes 5 and 11 are the exceptions, and both earn it by naming a catalyst and being careful about what it does or does not explain.
- Notes 1 and 12 remain byte identical, so the "mover with no verified catalyst" slot tests nothing the mover set already covers. A replacement, a mover whose only catalyst sits outside the move window, is queued and is not in pass 2.
- Note 8 compares 3.1 picks against 2 ranks when it says the market moved further of the two. It passes, because that comparison is what the assigned label asserts and the label is in the payload, but it is where the cross unit threshold question surfaces in generated text.
